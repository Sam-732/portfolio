Why this problem

  • Reach. For a first-time smartphone user, typing is the gate, not comprehension. Mixing Hindi and English is effortless spoken and slow typed.
  • Defence. Google ships voice in 25 Indian languages. OpenAI’s published India plans mention neither language nor voice.
  • The gap. A voice note is decoded by a human, who handles accent, noise and Hinglish. Voice mode needs a model to get it right first time, or the turn is wasted.

Who would use it, and for what

  • Students: exam preparation, assignments, code, talking an idea through.
  • Working professionals: research and drafting between other tasks, where speaking is faster than typing.
  • People more at ease in a local language than in English: asking in Hindi, Gujarati or a mix, without a keyboard.

Discoverability caps trial. Recognition quality caps retention, and it is the binding constraint

Friction points in ChatGPT mobile

  • Two icons, two products. The mic is dictation; the voice icon is spoken conversation. They sit side by side, unlabelled.
  • Inconsistent surface. Voice opens inline in the chat or as a separate full screen, depending on rollout.
  • Language is auto-detected by default, which is the failure case for someone switching between Hindi and English. The fix is three levels deep in settings.
  • Interruptions. OpenAI’s own advice is headphones or a quieter room, which is unrealistic in shared spaces.
  • No tools in voice. No image generation, no file upload.
  • Free tier runs a smaller voice model, and most users in India are on Free or Go.

Increasing voice mode adoption, ChatGPT mobile, IndiaMilestone 1, slide 3 of 3: KPI tree

Measured in successful tasks, not taps on the mic

North star
% of weekly active India users who complete at least one successful voice-originated task per week

Not voice sessions per user: that rises when the model mishears and the user repeats. Every metric here is measured among ChatGPT’s own weekly active users in India.

Adoption breadthVoice users as a share of ChatGPT’s weekly active users in India
Usage intensityVoice messages as a share of all messages, per voice user
Mic discoveryOf users shown the entry point, the share who tap it for the first time
Transcript success bottleneckShare of voice turns sent without editing, by language
First-use repeat the testOf first-time voice users, the share who return within 7 days of a successful voice turn
Output consumption bottleneckWhether the reply is heard or read, and reply length against typed turns
Task completionSession success for voice against typed, and how often users switch to typing mid-session
Session depthVoice turns per conversation, and time to second use after a first success

The test that decides the roadmap

Low trial, high retention means discoverability: surface the mic, which is cheap and fast. High trial, low retention means recognition: surfacing cannot fix it. Measure first-attempt rate and week 1 to week 4 voice retention together, split by language, before funding either.

Guardrails

Total messages per user must not fall: voice should add reach, not replace typing for people the keyboard already serves. Overall session success must hold: if pushing voice drags it down, stop promoting and start fixing.

Increasing voice mode adoption, ChatGPT mobile, IndiaMilestone 2, slide 3 of 3: problem framing canvas

The true problem: people who use voice go silent when others are around

1. Who is facing it?

Students and working professionals who already use voice mode in private, and spend their day in libraries, reading rooms and offices.

2. What is the true problem?

They won’t speak to ChatGPT aloud in a shared space, because they don’t want to disturb the people near them.

Asked why voice felt awkward, this was the reason underneath.

3. Why should we solve it?

The habit is already there: 25 of 36 people surveyed use voice somewhere. The setting is what stops them, and the potential base is 66 to 82.5 million users.

4. How do we know it’s real?

18 of 25 voice users named a concrete barrier, awkwardness in public among them. In interviews, users said they use voice when alone and stop in public settings.

5. Value for users

Voice becomes usable in the places where they actually study and work. Speaking a prompt is faster than typing it.

6. Value for business

Illustration: if 5% of 82 million potential users, about 4.1 million, took Go or Plus in equal numbers: 2.05M × ₹399 + 2.05M × ₹1,999, about ₹492 crore a month.

Also found, and set aside for now: mixed-language recognition for Hinglish and Gujarati; giving full context in one spoken request; long conversations drifting off topic.
